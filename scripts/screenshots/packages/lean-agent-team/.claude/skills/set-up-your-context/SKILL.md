---
name: Set Up Your Context
description: Check the three context files the team reads (company, person, voice) and, for any that are missing or still a template, interview me and write them. Use at the start of a conversation when those files are missing or unfilled, or when I ask to set up or redo my context.
---

Use this to give the team what it needs to know about me and my business. Every
agent reads `context/company.md`, `context/person.md` and `context/voice.md`
before acting. Without them, the team guesses.

## Step 1: check the files
Look at each of the three files and put it in one of three states:
- **Missing:** the file does not exist.
- **Unfilled:** it exists but still reads as a template: square-bracket
  placeholders such as `[Your name]`, or lines starting "Example:".
- **Filled:** it holds real answers. Leave it alone. An `[ADD: ...]` note is a
  gap I chose to leave, not a template, so a file with a few of them is filled.

Tell me in one line what you found, for example: "company.md is missing,
person.md and voice.md are filled." If all three are filled, say so and stop.
Otherwise ask whether I want to set up the missing ones now. If I say no, stop
and work with what there is.

## Step 2: interview me
Only for the files that are missing or unfilled. Ask two or three questions at a
time, wait for my answers, then ask the next few. Keep your own words short. If
an answer is vague, ask one follow-up, then move on.

For `company.md`:
1. In one or two sentences, what does your business do, and for whom?
2. Who is the one customer you most want more of? Their role, their situation,
   what they are trying to fix.
3. What do you sell, and roughly what does it cost? Your main one or two offers.

For `person.md`:
1. Your name and your role.
2. How do you like to work? For example: decide fast, want options not essays.
3. What do you care about most in how the team helps you?

For `voice.md`:
1. Three words a stranger should feel when they read your writing.
2. For each word, what it means in practice, and what its opposite looks like.
3. One line you or someone in your field might write that sounds flat and
   generic. I will rewrite it in your voice as the worked example.

## Step 3: write each file
Write one file at a time, in this shape, using only what I told you:

- `context/company.md`: a `# Company` heading, then sections "What we do",
  "Who we are for (ICP)" and "What we sell" (one line per offer, with price).
- `context/person.md`: an `# Owner` heading, then short bullets for Name, Role,
  How I work, and What I care about.
- `context/voice.md`: a `# Voice` heading, the three words, a short Do and
  Don't list, the read-it-aloud test, and a "Sounds like me" section with one
  pair: the generic line, then the same point in my voice.

Show me each file before you save it, and save it only when I say it is right.
Create the `context/` folder if it does not exist.

## Never
- Never overwrite a filled file without asking me first, by name.
- Never invent facts. Where I did not give you an answer, leave a clear
  [ADD: ...] note.
- Never touch anything outside these three files.
- Keep every file short. A few true lines beat a long template. No em dashes.
